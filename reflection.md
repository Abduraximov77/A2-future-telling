# Reflection: Porchlight

**Who is your future design meant to empower?**

Porchlight is meant to empower people who are often talked about but rarely designed for online: elders, new arrivals, and teenagers who carry knowledge that never shows up on a résumé or an app-store rating. In this world, Abuela Rosa's tamale-making counts as expertise, and a sixteen-year-old helping a neighbor video call his daughter counts as a real service. People trade hours instead of money, in whatever language feels like home.

**Why does this matter for you or your community?**

Much of today's internet is built to capture attention rather than build belonging. Neighbors can live a few feet apart and never speak, while platforms that promise connection measure success in clicks. Porchlight imagines the opposite: technology that gets out of the way and sends you toward a real doorstep. It matters because the people who most need community are often the ones least served by the tools we currently have.

**How do your design choices express this future?**

*Color:* The three pages move from dusk to evening to dawn, so walking through the site feels like a night on the porch that ends with a new day. Warm amber against deep purple is calmer than the harsh white of most screens.

*Layout:* Everything is built with CSS flexbox, so cards wrap and reflow gracefully on old phones and small screens. Large type and generous spacing make it easy to read.

*Navigation:* There are only three destinations, and each page ends with one clear "next door" link, so the path feels like walking from the gate to the porch to the workshop and back again.

*Imagery and language:* I used a simple glowing house instead of stock photos, and warm, plain language with greetings from several languages.

*Responsibility:* The Workshop page lists the designer's promises: no ads, no tracking, text-first, and accessible to keyboards and screen readers. Designers decide who gets included, so I wanted those commitments visible instead of hidden. Porchlight is still my vision, though. A real version would have to be shaped by the neighbors it hopes to serve.
